# The galaxy, planets and the sky

Part of the map of how Mutt is built (`CLAUDE.md`, Architecture, lists every file). Keep it current when this feature changes.

- `views/galaxy.js` is the main page: a sticky galaxy section followed by a scroll "runway". Scroll progress drives the zoom out into the universe stage (`track()`), and `/universe` opens already scrolled out. Stars (loose files) sit in a spiral round the void (`starSpots`); with a mouse they rest as points of light and open into cards lit from the void as the pointer comes near (`nearStars`, `.is-near`; `layout()` sets each one's `--near`, `--out`, `--tilt`); `DESIGN.md` "Stars".
- `planets.js` builds each planet: its kind (fourteen, `LOOKS`, with `lookOf()` picking one from the id when the owner hasn't; planets made before there were twelve keep drawing from the first eight, which the server marks with `twelve`; the painter's numbers are in `LOOK_INDEX`: Stardust and Twilight come after the moons, 22 and 23). Type and colour are one box (`pickLook` in `views/planet.js`): pressing the chosen type again steps the planet's colour through `PALETTE`, palette, moons, how used it is (`use` from `planet_use` in `mutt/api.py`: weather when busy, dust when forgotten, the dust lifting when a file lands), rings at ten files, and a texture of its files as cards in a belt, each drawn as what it is (`FACES`, `faceOf`). `DESIGN.md` "Planets" has the kinds, how to add one, and how a new kind of file or mode gets its drawing on a world. `planetgl.js` paints them: one shared off-screen WebGL2 canvas draws every planet's sphere (ground, weather and light in one shader, branching on the kind) and copies it onto the planet's own canvas, about 30 times a second each. Without WebGL2 (or with `?flat=1`) the older 2D painter takes over.
- Planet positions: `drawers.x`/`y` are offsets from the void in desktop pixels (empty means "tidy it"), saved by `PUT /api/galaxy/layout`. `scatter()` in `views/galaxy.js` places the saved ones first; unsaved ones go on the orbits round the void (`orbitLayout`, `ring`, `openSpots`). A dragged planet snaps to a free place on an orbit (`openFor`, `snapTo`, shown by `.gx-guide`/`.gx-ghost`), and the galaxy keeps its size while you arrange (`held`). `fitted()` picks the largest size that fits. `movable()` in `drag.js` does the dragging. The universe is rows with you in the middle (`spreadUniverse`): galaxies you're drawn to take the places nearest yours, the strongest pull closest, and everyone else fills the rest A to Z. The pull is `nearness` (per person, private to them): a visit to someone's galaxy or one of their planets adds 1 (once an hour), saving one of their files adds 3, and it halves every 30 days (`draw_near`, `nearness_of` in `mutt/api.py`; sent as `near` in `/api/galaxies`).
- The void's slow sums (its cells, its tile of dots) live in `voidmake.js`, which may not touch the page, and run in `void-worker.js`; `voidart.js` asks for them once and draws when they are in. The void itself is a stack of seven canvases the browser lays over each other; nothing is copied from one canvas onto another (`DESIGN.md` "Print: the void").
- `sky.js` is the fixed background: the ground, the moving print dust (two small programs on a WebGL2 canvas, eight pictures a second, still on a slow computer), and the stars as their own layer. Pages can give it a lens (the void bends stars), a pull (stars lean toward a point) and a night amount (the universe: black, no stars). `DESIGN.md` "The sky" has its look and its cost rules; keep every one when changing it. Out in the universe, `uvweb.js` paints the stippled web once and `beads()` sends small lights along its threads.
- The drop ring (round whatever has `.is-target`, the planet ghost and the void's `.drop-ring`; `DESIGN.md` "Drop ring") is a loop of dust: `dropring.js` paints it once at start (`dustLoop` in `uvweb.js` + a dot tile from `voidmake.js` `evenPoints`) into `--dust-loop` / `--dust-dots` and sets `html.has-dust`; the one rule headed THE DROP RING in `galaxy.css` masks `--thread-on` with them. A new drop target gets it by adding its selector there and a reach of its own.
