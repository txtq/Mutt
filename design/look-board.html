The sky behind the galaxy and the universe

Today's sky is four stock pieces: a smooth blue gradient, two soft clouds of colour, white dots scattered evenly, and thin straight-edged lines. Any space-themed page could have it. The void is the opposite: nobody else has that stippled print. So the question is what the sky should be made of, so that it is Mutt's.

Each option shows your galaxy on the left and the universe on the right. The void and planets are rough stand-ins, only there for scale; judge the sky. These can be mixed: "B, with A's threads low down" is a fair answer.

Choice 9What is the sky made of?

Gradient, colour clouds, even dots, angular lines. In the universe: black, dots and a faint web of straight lines.

The Vita's waves, drawn the way the Vita drew them: bundles of fine lines that flow, pinch together and fan apart, and glow where they gather. No colour clouds, fewer and finer stars. In the universe the same threads thin out and run between galaxies. The safest step: plainly Vita, and it still moves the way the sky moves now.

The void's own language carried across the whole sky: light made of fine dots, thick along one band and toward the bright floor, with dark winding lanes through it like the void's cage. Dust gathers round the void. In the universe it goes fully black and white, each galaxy a knot of dots, so planets and files are the only colour anywhere. Nothing else looks like it, and it makes the void belong to its sky.

The calmest: a nearly flat deep sky, a faint band of stars, and the orbits your planets already sit on drawn as hairlines round the void. It shows how a galaxy is arranged, and every galaxy in the universe gets its own small rings. The risk is that it reads as a diagram.

Choice 10How bright is the bottom of the sky?

The electric blue floor. The most Vita, and the reason small words need a shadow to survive down there.

Small words on a bright sky

The sky gets brighter toward the bottom, and that is where captions end up after you scroll. Today's two faintest greys disappear there. The small number after each word is how strongly it stands out from the sky behind it: small words want 4.5 or more, large ones 3.

Choice 1How do captions, dates and hints stay readable?

File names and dates sink to 2.0 on the bright sky and "Add a note" to 1.0, which is invisible.

Every grey steps up, and the faintest one is no longer used for words at all. Better everywhere, still thin in the brightest corner.

The same greys, and words that sit straight on the sky get the soft dark shadow planet names already have. The numbers don't count the shadow; your eye does. No grey alone can pass in that corner without turning white.

The planet'sonly on its own things

The rule for a planet's colour is simple enough to check by eye: if it sits on box glass on the planet's page, it is the planet's and may take its colour. If it sits on bar glass (the top bar, sheets, menus, dialogs, toasts), it is Mutt's and stays blue. A planet's button is one flat tint of its colour under the same gloss, never a two-colour gradient.

Choice 2How much of the planet's colour shows on a box at rest?

A coloured edge and a halo on every box, all the time. With a page full of boxes the glow stops meaning anything.

Plain glass until you point. The calmest, but every planet's page looks alike until you move the pointer.

A hairline of the planet's colour and no halo. Pointing at a box brightens its edge and brings the glow, so the glow becomes a signal. The page still feels like that planet.

The two glasses

Box glass holds your things: light, flat, see-through. Bar glass holds Mutt's: darker, for the top bar, menus, sheets, dialogs and toasts. Today there are three recipes and 17 strengths of frost; two of the recipes are gradients, which your own rule excludes. Each sample sits over the same bright sky, a ribbon and a planet so you can see what the frost does to them.

Choice 4How strong should the frost and the edge be?

Box glass brightens the sky behind it, which is why words wash out low on the page. Menus and sheets are a dark gradient.

Thinnest. You see the sky sharply through it, and ribbons cut straight across your words.

A deeper frost and a slightly firmer edge. The sky behind goes soft and a touch deeper, so words hold anywhere on the page. Bar glass becomes one flat dark sheet.

The most solid and the easiest to read, but it starts to hide the sky, and the sky is what makes it Mutt.

Gloss

Gloss is for things you press and nothing else. Today the shine is drawn four ways: one on the blue button, another on the plain button, a brighter one on the chosen segment, and a hand-mixed one on Delete. The proposal is one light, from above, laid over whatever colour the control is. Try them: they press, switch and slide.

Choice 5How glossy should the Vita controls be?

Four shines that nearly agree. Look at the switch beside the chosen segment.

A soft sheen with no line across the middle. Quiet and current, but it stops being the Vita.

A bright lip on the top edge and a sheen over the upper half that fades at the middle instead of stopping at a hard line. Plainly Vita, not a toy.

The full glossy bubble with a hard line. The most nostalgic, and it reads cartoony beside the stippled void.

Paper

Paper is for things that are writing: documents, book pages, the little pages on planets. One cream instead of three, flat, with square-ish corners so it never looks like glass. A serif appears on paper and nowhere else. Today documents use one serif and the book reader another, so the same paragraph looks different in the two.

Choice 6Which serif for everything on paper?

Already what documents use. Looks the same on every device, has real italics, and tightens itself for titles and opens up for small text.

What the book reader uses now. Nothing to fetch, but a Mac shows Iowan and Windows shows Palatino or Georgia, so pages differ between devices.

Made for long reading on screens. Sturdier in small sizes, plainer in titles, and one more typeface to fetch.

Four heights

Four shadows, named by how far a thing is lifted off the sky, replace today's 94. One shadow colour, the night blue of the top of the sky. On a dark sky shadows are quiet by nature; the edge and the frost do most of the lifting.
