Quotes in Mutt

A note that reads as a quote (words in quotation marks, or a line ending “— Someone”) already gets noticed by Mutt: autosort sends it to a Quotes planet. Today it still shows as a plain note. Below are the ways it could be shown, with my pick marked in amber. The planet here is an amber one to show where its colour lands.

1 · Its box, wherever it is

A quote’s file box

Switches on by itself for any note that reads as a quote, like a song gets its level bars. The words are set larger the shorter they are (three sizes), and who said it and where from sit underneath, read out of the note. The note itself is not changed. A quote too long for its box fades out at the foot, like a note.

5 · Underneath

Reading who said it

  • Today Mutt misses a lot of quotes. “— Mary Oliver, Wild Geese” doesn’t count, because of the comma; nor does a quote longer than about a paragraph. I’d widen what it reads: “— Name, Source”, “— Name (Film, 1999)”, “Name, in Source”, a source on its own line, a link under the quote (its page’s title becomes the source), and quotes up to about 1,200 characters when they’re in quotation marks.
  • One reader, on the server: the same check autosort uses splits a quote into its words, who said it and where from, and sends that with the note. Nothing new is stored; edit the note anywhere and it reads again.
  • If Mutt gets one wrong, the file’s menu gets “Show as a note” (remembered on that file), and “Show as a quote” to put it back.
  • While typing, nothing new: the box simply turns into a quote once it’s added. I’d skip a “this looks like a quote” line while you write; it would be noise.
What I’d build now

My recommendation

  • Now: reading who said it (5), the quote box (1, look C, or A/B if you’d rather not add the serif), the file view large on the sky (2A) with Copy.
  • Now too, if you want a mode: the commonplace book (3) with the wall and “By who”. The reading room one at a time could follow later.
  • Later: Save as picture (4).
